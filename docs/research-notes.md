**The pattern:** In Replit, Amazon Q, GitHub MCP, and PocketOS, the damage came from a *tool call* (DB drop, `rm`, cloud delete, PR creation) that no policy or human checked before it ran. ActionGuard intercepts exactly that layer.

## 4. Standards and frameworks

- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025**
  - **LLM01 Prompt Injection** is ranked #1.
  - **LLM06 Excessive Agency** is "the vulnerability that enables damaging actions to be performed in response to unexpected, ambiguous or manipulated outputs from an LLM." It names three root causes: excessive functionality, excessive permissions, and excessive autonomy.
  - Its mitigations are almost exactly ActionGuard's design: *"Utilise human-in-the-loop control to require a human to approve high-impact actions before they are taken"* and *"Implement authorization in downstream systems rather than relying on an LLM to decide if an action is allowed."*
  - Source: https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/
- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026** (published 2026-08-03):
  - **Excessive Agency rose from #6 to LLM03.** Prompt Injection stays at LLM01.
  - The methodology weights practitioner votes at 75% and data from 6,639 real incidents at 25%.
  - OWASP also unveiled an **Agent Control Standard (ACS) v0.1**, an open spec for *runtime enforcement* of agents with policy enforcement points and an Agent Bill of Materials, on Sep 1-2, 2026.
  - Sources: https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/ ; CSA research note, 2026-09-04: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-owasp-genai-top10-2026-agent-control-stand/
- **OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026** (published 2025-12-09):
  - The list: ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack, **ASI02 Tool Misuse & Exploitation**, **ASI03 Identity & Privilege Abuse**, ASI04 Agentic Supply Chain, **ASI05 Unexpected Code Execution**, ASI06 Memory & Context Poisoning, ASI07 Insecure Inter-Agent Comms, ASI08 Cascading Failures, **ASI09 Human-Agent Trust Exploitation**, ASI10 Rogue Agents.
  - Sources: https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/ ; list via https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/
- **NIST**
  - AI RMF 1.0 (AI 100-1, Jan 2023) defines four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
  - CAISI launched the **AI Agent Standards Initiative** on 2026-02-17, covering agent security, identity, and authorization. It included an RFI on AI agent security and an NCCoE concept paper on agent identity and authorization. https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/announcing-ai-agent-standards-initiative-interoperable-and-secure
- **EU AI Act**
  - **Art. 14 (Human oversight):** High-risk systems must be designed so humans can oversee them, understand their limits, avoid automation bias, override or disregard outputs, and stop the system with a "stop" button or similar procedure. https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-14
  - **Timeline after the Digital Omnibus:**
    - Prohibitions have applied since 2025-02-02.
    - GPAI obligations have applied since 2025-08-02.
    - Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2026-08-02.
    - **High-risk obligations for Annex III (stand-alone) systems are deferred to 2027-12-02, and for Annex I (product-embedded) systems to 2028-08-02.**
    - The provisional deal was reached 2026-05-07. Parliament voted 2026-06-16, the Council approved 2026-06-29, and the act was published in the OJ on 2026-07-24.
    - Sources: Sidley, 2026-06-22: https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/ ; FPF: https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/
- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** is the first certifiable AI management system standard, published 2023-12-18. https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/90574

**Where ActionGuard fits:** Most acquired products work at the **content layer**: they classify prompts and outputs. The incidents in §3 happened at the **action layer**, where a valid-looking tool call caused the damage. OWASP (LLM06/LLM03 mitigations, ASI02/ASI03, and the 2026 Agent Control Standard) and Gartner ("enforced guardrails, rollback, circuit breakers"; 13% governance readiness) both point at runtime action enforcement.

ActionGuard combines three things:
1. A deterministic policy plus risk score on every tool call (shell, file, HTTP, DB, payment, email).
2. **Escalation to a human** that meets EU AI Act Art. 14 oversight expectations.
3. A **tamper-evident (hash-chained) audit log**.

Among the products above, Invariant (policy) and HumanLayer (approvals) each cover part of this; neither combines all three.
